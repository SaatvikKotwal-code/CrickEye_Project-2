\chapter*{Abstract}

\par Cricket is the most popular sport in India, yet access to professional coaching remains heavily concentrated in metropolitan centres. Academies in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities face a significant gap: they lack the expensive sensor arrays, radar equipment, and expert video analysts that top-tier facilities use to evaluate batting technique. This project, \textbf{CrickEye}, addresses that gap by delivering a low-cost, computer-vision-based cricket coaching system that analyses net-session footage captured from a single, front-on camera — the kind of setup any academy can afford.

\par CrickEye is built around a multi-stage deep learning pipeline. In the first pass, the system processes every video frame through a YOLOv8 pose-estimation model to extract seventeen COCO-standard body keypoints — shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles, and head landmarks — for the primary batter in the frame. From these keypoints, per-frame bilateral wrist velocities are computed and a swing-detection algorithm identifies individual shot deliveries by locating sustained high-velocity windows that satisfy minimum duration, frame-fraction, and travel constraints. Each detected swing is then passed to a Swin Transformer 3D (Swin3D-T) video classifier trained on five cricket shot classes — cover drive, flick, pull, straight drive, and sweep — using clips collected and curated from publicly available YouTube cricket footage.

\par For every confirmed shot, CrickEye extracts a comprehensive set of biomechanical metrics designed specifically for front-on camera reliability. Head quality is scored by tracking nose or ear-midpoint stability across the shot window relative to shoulder width, with shot-type-specific rules (e.g. a pull allows more vertical tracking than a straight drive). Batting stance symmetry is assessed by measuring shoulder and hip tilt angles during quiet pre-swing frames. Elbow shape is evaluated by comparing elbow spread ratios at setup versus contact, flagging cramped or over-reaching arm positions. Footwork analysis detects pre-delivery foot movement (trigger) and front-foot plant timing relative to the moment of contact, differentiating between front-foot and back-foot shot types. Swing intensity provides a session-relative effort measure using the 90th percentile of rolling-mean wrist velocity, while bat speed is estimated by calibrating pixel displacement against shoulder width as a physical reference.

\par These five metrics are combined into a weighted composite shot score (Head 35\%, Footwork 25\%, Stance Symmetry 15\%, Elbow Shape 15\%, Swing Intensity 10\%) that produces a 0--10 rating per delivery. An optional Ball Analytics module adds YOLO-based ball tracking for delivery speed estimation, bounce detection via 2D path curvature analysis, and length-zone classification (yorker, full, good length, short).

\par The system is deployed as a web application with a FastAPI backend that processes uploads via WebSocket-streamed real-time progress updates, and a coaching-first frontend dashboard featuring per-shot cards, session summaries, wagon-wheel visualisations, pitch maps, and automated coaching alerts with drills. Role-based authentication supports both player and coach views. A session-level analysis engine aggregates metrics across all deliveries, detects fatigue through second-half swing-intensity drop-off, and generates prioritised coaching alerts tied to repeated biomechanical patterns.

\par The dataset for training the Swin3D shot classifier was collected by extracting and manually labelling clips from publicly available YouTube cricket videos, and all testing was likewise performed on YouTube-sourced net-session footage. Results demonstrate that CrickEye provides actionable, consistent biomechanical feedback from commodity hardware, offering Tier-2 and Tier-3 cricket academies a practical AI coaching tool that operates without specialised sensors, high-speed cameras, or expensive infrastructure.

\par \textbf{Keywords:} Cricket coaching, computer vision, pose estimation, YOLOv8, Swin Transformer 3D, shot classification, biomechanical analysis, low-cost sports analytics, front-on camera, deep learning.
